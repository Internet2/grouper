| ### **From Zoom AI Call Summary**  **AI Tools for Grouper Management**  Chris discussed several AI-powered tools and scripts for managing group memberships in Grouper, including a proof of concept for calling ChatGPT via web service to streamline processes within the UI. He shared examples of automating membership additions using GSH scripts, highlighting challenges and improvements in coding practices. Chris also demonstrated how AI can be trained to generate web service requests and correct API calls, and he explored creating institutional training files to provide version-specific guidance for Grouper tasks. The discussion concluded with ideas for standardizing documentation and training across institutions, potentially simplifying the process of integrating AI into institutional-specific applications.  **AI Integration in Grouper Development**  The team discussed their experiences with AI and coding, with Chris proposing to implement web services in Grouper with the help of an intern this summer. Drew shared his use of AI to review infrastructure code, while Chad expressed caution about AI's ability to generate convincing but incorrect code. Jim described using AI for log analysis and programming assistance, noting its potential for generating helpful methods. Shilen inquired about the amount of information needed to train AI for Grouper-related tasks, prompting Chris to suggest starting with web services as a quick win.  **AI Training Data Structure Discussion**  Chris and Shilen discussed the process of creating and editing files for AI training, focusing on how to structure data inputs and manage AI behavior. Chris explained the concept of using a GSH template with specific inputs for group operations, such as left and right factors, and emphasized the need to align the AI's understanding with the desired outcomes. They also touched on the importance of context in web service cases and UI examples, where the AI needs to recognize specific group actions. Chris noted that while there are some institution-specific requirements, the overall approach involves ensuring the AI can interpret and execute the necessary operations effectively.  **AI Training With Knowledge Files**  Chris explained how knowledge files are used to train AI models to understand and process organizational data, particularly for tasks like navigating group pages and managing user memberships. He demonstrated the process of creating and updating these files, emphasizing the need for periodic retraining or updates to maintain accuracy. Chris also shared examples of training files, including database queries, organizational structures, and user data, highlighting the importance of structured data for effective AI model training.  **AI Data Privacy and Group Analysis**  The team discussed the use of AI and data privacy, particularly regarding supervisor and group information. Chris demonstrated a GPT prompt for retrieving direct reports, though the results were not entirely accurate. Shilen and Chris discussed the security of inputting organizational data into AI systems, with Chris explaining that their institution has a private training environment with appropriate controls. The conversation shifted to potential AI projects for the summer, with Chad suggesting role mining to identify commonalities in manual groups for creating reference groups. Chris showed a GSH template that analyzes groups for organizational membership and provides recommendations for automating group memberships.  **AI Integration in Grouper Services**  The team discussed integrating AI capabilities into Grouper, starting with web services and GSH templates. Chris demonstrated a working example of using AI to generate code snippets and explained plans to implement similar functionality for ABAC GSH. Shilen expressed interest but noted the need to consider acceptable usage of AI within their institution. The group agreed to explore adding a screen for web service examples under the miscellaneous section, with Chris planning to post more details later. |
