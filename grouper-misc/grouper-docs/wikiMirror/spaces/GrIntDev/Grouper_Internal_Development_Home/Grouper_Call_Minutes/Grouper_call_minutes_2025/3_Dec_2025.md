### **Discussion Summary from Zoom AI**

**Community Provisioner Contributions Discussion**

The group discussed encouraging community-driven contributions of provisioners, with Chris Hyzer and others agreeing that while institution-specific implementations can be shared, there's no requirement for complete genericization. They discussed the value of sharing examples like Penn's LogicGate and BPLogix implementations, with Emily suggesting that community contributions should be highlighted at TechX events. Chad shared details about CCure-specific provisioner development, noting that while some API calls are institution-specific, the underlying logic is generic, and he agreed to update the wiki with improvements while keeping the Secure-specific content separate.

**Conference Planning and Grouper Training**

The team discussed who would attend and present at an upcoming conference, with several members unable to attend due to budget constraints. Chris Hyzer announced plans for a pre-conference seminar on Grouper and AI, which will feature presentations on MCPs and maturity levels by Jim and Chris. They also discussed the possibility of creating recordings and an advanced track for Grouper training on AI, as well as developing a dedicated provisioning module by the end of the year.

**Wiki Improvements for Lifecycle Events**

The team discussed improvements to the wiki for generic provisioners and agreed to collaborate on editing it. Shilen presented updates on the new Lifecycle Events page, which includes configuration for privileged and unprivileged JEXO scripts. Chris Hyzer explained that lifecycle events can involve multiple simultaneous actions, such as changing bosses, groups, and leaving the organization, and suggested simplifying the display by showing the most important event. Bert suggested that seeing both privileged and unprivileged views could be helpful for explaining events to others.

**User Lifecycle Events Display Strategy**

The team discussed how to display and manage user lifecycle events in group management. They agreed to show only the soonest upcoming event per user, prioritizing the date over event magnitude. Chris suggested this would be simpler for users and easier to implement. Bert and Chris clarified that these are manual policy groups, not reference groups, and explained the process of re-granting access based on enterprise data or accelerating removal. Shilen was tasked with describing this process at the top of the screen.

**User Group Membership Lifecycle Management**

The team discussed user lifecycle management and group membership rules, focusing on how to handle users who should remain in a group despite eligibility changes. Chris explained that the current MVP feature allows administrators to "keep" selected users in a group by overriding their lifecycle events, while Bert and Carey explored the implications of eligibility rules and automatic removals. They discussed the need for clear documentation and rules around grace periods and membership requirements, with Chris noting that while the MVP implementation focuses on basic functionality, more complex features like eligibility vetoes and end dates could be added later.

**Grouper Upgrade and Training Review**

The team discussed the upcoming upgrade to a new capability planned for the first week of February, with testing potentially starting in January. Chris Hyzer confirmed that the necessary testing was nearly complete, and Shillin agreed there were no issues. They also reviewed the success of a recent Grouper training session, which had over 20 participants and went smoothly without any major bugs. Chad mentioned he would work on fixing some minor issues in the on-demand GTE. Finally, Chad reported on his work analyzing the Grouper MISC AI directory, noting that some of the information seemed outdated.

**Grouper Knowledge Base Conversion**

The team discussed the challenges of converting the Grouper wiki into a knowledge base format, with Chad explaining his manual process of selecting 180 quality wiki pages for conversion to Markdown. They explored using Olama as a knowledge retrieval tool, though Chad noted limited success with the current implementation. The group agreed to consider adding an AI search feature to the Grouper UI, with Carey suggesting a minimal viable product approach using a simple help button that opens in a new tab. Jim demonstrated a separate project involving a reverse proxy with chatbot functionality that interfaces with a RAG knowledge base backend, though this remains a separate initiative from the Grouper project.
