# GRP-7509  Contrast on direct/indirect privilege buttons harder to see than before

In [https://todos.grouper.internet2.edu/browse/GRP-7210|https://todos.grouper.internet2.edu/browse/GRP-7210|smart-link], the privilege direct vs. indirect buttons were made darker (#555555->#000000 and #cccccc → #767676) to make a larger contrast from the background. The ratio of their contrast is still the same, but is harder to visually detect because they are both darker.

Due to WCAG constraints on contrast, the light color can’t be too light. Considering privilege rows alternate between white and #f5f5f5. the lightest feasible color that passes is #888888, or #858585 as a safer choice. It’s still harder to tell an indirect vs direct checkbox than before, but it’s slightly better.

-To improve visibility even more, the direct checkbox could have a bolder weight added to it.-

### Chad Redman - 2026-10-20T13:49:03.173-0400

These options work with icon fonts:

# *Different glyph for direct and indirect.* This is the strongest option because it doesn't depend on color at all. Examples are a solid {{fa-check-circle}} for direct and a hollow {{fa-circle-check}} in the Regular style for indirect, or a plain check for indirect and a check inside a filled square for direct. Regular glyphs need {{font-weight: 400}} with {{font-family: "Font Awesome 6 Free"}}. Only some FA6 Regular glyphs are in the free set, so I'd check which ones are available before choosing.
# *Size.* Make the direct check slightly larger, for example {{font-size: 1.15em}}. It's easy to apply and noticeable.
# *A thin outline of the same color.* {{-webkit-text-stroke: 0.5px #000}} on the direct check does thicken the glyph, but support and rendering vary, so it's the least robust.
# *A background or ring on the direct state.* For example, a small filled circle in a dark color with a white check. This also gives a larger click target.
